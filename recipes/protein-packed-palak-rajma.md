## Ingredients

- 2 cans (400 g each) kidney beans (rajma), drained and rinsed — or 1½ cups dried rajma soaked overnight, pressure-cooked until tender
- 250 g fresh spinach (palak), washed and roughly chopped
- 150 g paneer, cut into 1 cm cubes (optional — see notes)
- 2 tbsp neutral oil or ghee
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 2 medium onions, finely chopped
- 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
- 2 medium tomatoes, finely chopped (or ¾ cup canned crushed tomatoes)
- 1 tsp coriander powder
- 1 tsp cumin powder
- ½ tsp turmeric powder
- 1 tsp Kashmiri red chilli powder (mild heat, deep colour)
- 1 tsp garam masala
- Salt, to taste
- ½ cup water (adjust for consistency)
- 2 tbsp fresh cream or plain yogurt (optional, to finish)
- Fresh coriander leaves, to garnish

## Steps

1. **Blanch the spinach.** Bring a pot of water to a boil, add the spinach, and cook for 1–2 minutes until wilted. Transfer immediately to a bowl of cold water to preserve the colour, then drain. Blend to a smooth purée and set aside.
2. **Temper the spices (tadka).** Heat the oil or ghee in a deep pan or kadhai over medium heat. Add the cumin seeds and let them sizzle for 20–30 seconds until fragrant — this is called a tadka, and it blooms the spices in fat to build deep flavour.
3. **Cook the onions.** Add the chopped onions and sauté, stirring frequently, for 8–10 minutes until they turn golden brown. Don't rush this step — well-cooked onions are the base of the gravy.
4. **Add ginger-garlic paste.** Stir in the ginger-garlic paste and cook for 2 minutes until the raw smell disappears.
5. **Add tomatoes and dry spices.** Add the chopped tomatoes, coriander powder, cumin powder, turmeric, and Kashmiri chilli powder. Cook on medium heat for 6–8 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the tomatoes break down and the oil begins to separate from the masala.
6. **Add the spinach purée.** Pour in the blended spinach and stir well to combine with the masala. Cook for 3–4 minutes on medium heat.
7. **Add the kidney beans.** Tip in the drained kidney beans along with the water. Stir, season with salt, and simmer uncovered for 8–10 minutes so the beans absorb the flavours. Lightly mash a few beans against the side of the pan to naturally thicken the gravy.
8. **Add paneer (if using).** Gently fold in the paneer cubes. Simmer for 2–3 minutes — enough to warm them through without breaking them apart.
9. **Finish with garam masala.** Sprinkle over the garam masala and stir through. If using cream or yogurt, swirl it in now and cook for 1 more minute.
10. **Taste and serve.** Adjust salt, then ladle into bowls. Garnish with fresh coriander. Serve hot with steamed rice, roti, or naan.

## Notes

- **Why this beats meat for weeknight protein:** A single serving delivers roughly 18 g of plant protein — comparable to a small chicken portion — plus 12 g of fibre that most meat dishes can't match. The combination of legumes (rajma) and greens (palak) also provides iron, folate, and magnesium.
- **New to vegetarian cooking?** Rajma is one of the most satisfying meat-free mains because kidney beans have a meaty, creamy texture that holds up in bold gravies. This dish is a great starting point.
- **Canned beans shortcut:** Using canned kidney beans brings total time to under 1 hour with zero soaking. Dried beans need overnight soaking + 20–25 minutes in a pressure cooker (or 45–60 min in a regular pot) before step 6.
- **Make it vegan:** Skip the paneer and swap ghee for oil, cream for coconut cream or just leave it out. The dish is fully satisfying without either.
- **Leftovers:** The gravy thickens as it cools. Add a splash of water when reheating. Tastes even better the next day.
