## Ingredients

### For the Cheela Batter
- 1 cup (200 g) split yellow moong dal (dhuli moong dal), soaked for 4 hours
- 1 medium green chilli, roughly chopped
- 1-inch piece of fresh ginger, peeled
- ½ tsp cumin seeds (jeera)
- ¼ tsp turmeric powder (haldi)
- ½ tsp salt, or to taste
- ½ cup water (adjust for batter consistency)
- 2 tbsp finely chopped fresh coriander (cilantro)
- 1 small onion, finely chopped
- 1 small tomato, finely chopped and deseeded
- 1 tsp oil per cheela, for cooking

### For the Mint Curd Dip
- 1 cup plain full-fat curd (yogurt / dahi), whisked smooth
- 10–12 fresh mint leaves (pudina), finely chopped
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander leaves, finely chopped
- ½ tsp roasted cumin powder (bhuna jeera)
- ¼ tsp black salt (kala namak) — optional but traditional
- Salt to taste
- Pinch of red chilli powder for garnish

## Steps

1. **Soak the dal.** Rinse the moong dal in cold water 2–3 times until the water runs clear. Cover with fresh water and soak for at least 4 hours (or overnight in the fridge). Drain before using.

2. **Blend the batter.** Add the drained moong dal, green chilli, ginger, cumin seeds, turmeric, and salt to a blender. Add ¼ cup of water and blend until you have a smooth, thick batter — similar to pancake batter. Add the remaining water only if needed; the batter should coat the back of a spoon.

3. **Fold in the vegetables.** Transfer the batter to a bowl. Stir in the chopped onion, tomato, and coriander. These add texture and freshness to each cheela.

4. **Make the mint curd dip.** In a small bowl, whisk the curd until lump-free. Mix in the mint, coriander, roasted cumin powder, black salt (if using), and regular salt to taste. Sprinkle a pinch of red chilli powder on top and refrigerate until serving.

5. **Heat the tawa (griddle).** Place a flat non-stick pan or cast-iron tawa over medium-high heat. Let it get properly hot — a drop of water should sizzle and evaporate immediately. Lightly grease with ½ tsp oil and wipe away the excess with a folded paper towel (this ensures the batter spreads evenly without sticking).

6. **Pour and spread the batter.** Pour a large ladle (about ¼ cup) of batter onto the centre of the tawa. Using the back of the ladle, spread it outward in a gentle circular motion to form a thin, round cheela about 6–7 inches in diameter. Work quickly — the batter sets fast.

7. **Cook the first side.** Drizzle ½ tsp oil around the edges of the cheela. Cook on medium heat for 2–3 minutes until the top surface looks dry and matte (no wet batter visible) and the underside is golden-brown. You can lift an edge with a spatula to check.

8. **Flip and finish.** Flip the cheela carefully and cook the second side for 1–2 minutes until light golden. The cheela is done when it is crisp at the edges and cooked through. Transfer to a plate.

9. **Repeat.** Re-grease the tawa lightly between each cheela and repeat steps 6–8 for the remaining batter. This recipe makes approximately 8 cheelas.

10. **Serve immediately.** Stack the hot cheelas and serve at once with the chilled mint curd dip on the side. Cheelas lose their crispness if they sit, so eat them fresh off the tawa.

## Notes

- **Soaking is non-negotiable.** Under-soaked dal won't blend smooth and the cheela will be dense. If you're short on time, soak in warm water for 2 hours.
- **Batter thickness matters.** Too thick → cheela won't spread thin; too thin → it tears when you flip. Aim for a pourable but not watery consistency.
- **Tawa temperature.** A cold pan is the #1 reason cheelas stick. Always preheat thoroughly. If the cheela tears while flipping, the tawa may not be hot enough, or the first side needs another 30 seconds.
- **Variations.** Add 50 g of crumbled paneer (Indian cottage cheese) to the batter for an extra protein hit, as shown in the reference video. You can also fold in grated carrot or baby spinach.
- **Make-ahead.** The batter can be blended and refrigerated (without the vegetables) for up to 24 hours. Stir in the veggies just before cooking. The mint curd dip keeps for up to 2 days covered in the fridge.
- **Gluten-free:** Moong dal is naturally gluten-free, making this a safe option for those avoiding wheat.
