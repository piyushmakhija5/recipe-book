## Ingredients

### For the Masoor Dal
- 1 cup red masoor dal (split red lentils), rinsed well
- 2½ cups water
- 1 medium tomato, finely chopped
- 1 medium onion, finely chopped
- 1 tsp ginger-garlic paste (or ½ tsp each grated ginger and garlic)
- 1 green chilli, slit (optional, skip if you prefer mild)
- ½ tsp turmeric powder
- ½ tsp red chilli powder
- ½ tsp coriander powder
- Salt to taste
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander (cilantro) leaves, chopped

### For the Tadka (Tempering)
- 1 tbsp ghee or oil
- ½ tsp cumin seeds
- 1 dried red chilli
- A pinch of asafoetida (hing)
- ½ tsp Kashmiri red chilli powder (for colour)

### For the Whole Wheat Roti (makes 6 rotis)
- 1½ cups whole wheat flour (atta), plus extra for dusting
- Water, as needed (roughly ½ cup)
- A pinch of salt
- ½ tsp oil or ghee (for kneading)

## Steps

### Making the Dal
1. Rinse the masoor dal in a bowl under running water 2–3 times, until the water runs mostly clear. This removes excess starch and any dust.
2. Place the rinsed dal in a pressure cooker or saucepan. Add 2½ cups of water and ½ tsp turmeric. If using a pressure cooker, cook on medium heat for 2 whistles (about 8 minutes). If using a saucepan, simmer covered for 20–25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils are completely soft and mushy.
3. While the dal cooks, heat 1 tsp oil in a separate pan over medium heat. Add the chopped onion and sauté, stirring frequently, for 5–6 minutes until it turns golden.
4. Add the ginger-garlic paste and the slit green chilli to the onions. Stir and cook for 1–2 minutes until the raw smell disappears.
5. Add the chopped tomato, red chilli powder, and coriander powder. Mix well and cook for 4–5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the tomato softens and the oil begins to separate at the edges — this means the masala is cooked through.
6. Once the dal is done (and pressure has released if using a cooker), open the lid and stir. The lentils should be completely soft — mash a few with the back of a spoon to confirm.
7. Pour the cooked dal into the tomato-onion masala (or vice versa). Add salt to taste and stir well. Simmer together on low heat for 5 minutes so the flavours meld. Add a splash of water if the dal is too thick.
8. Turn off the heat and stir in the chopped coriander leaves.

### Making the Tadka
9. Heat 1 tbsp ghee or oil in a small pan (a tadka pan or any small vessel) over medium-high heat until it shimmers.
10. Add the cumin seeds — they should splutter immediately. Then add the dried red chilli and a pinch of hing. Let them sizzle for 10–15 seconds. Turn off the heat and add the Kashmiri red chilli powder right away (it can burn quickly off heat).
11. Pour this sizzling tadka immediately over the dal and stir gently. The smoky, spiced oil is what makes this dish shine.

### Making the Whole Wheat Roti
12. In a large bowl, mix the whole wheat flour with a pinch of salt. Add water a little at a time, mixing with your hand, until the dough just comes together. It should be soft and pliable, not sticky. Drizzle in the oil and knead for 2–3 minutes until smooth. Cover with a damp cloth and rest for 10 minutes (this makes the rotis softer).
13. Divide the dough into 6 equal balls, roughly the size of a golf ball.
14. Lightly flour your rolling surface. Using a rolling pin, roll one dough ball into a thin circle, about 6–7 inches across. Keep rotating the dough as you roll to keep it even and circular — don't worry if it's not perfect.
15. Heat a tawa (flat griddle) or a heavy skillet on high heat. Place a rolled roti on the dry tawa. Cook for about 30–40 seconds until you see small bubbles forming on top.
16. Flip the roti using tongs. Cook the second side for 30–40 seconds. You should see light brown spots appearing.
17. Flip once more and press gently with a folded cloth or a spatula — the roti should puff up. This takes about 20–30 seconds. Remove and apply a light brush of ghee if desired. Repeat with the remaining dough balls.

## Notes

- **Packing for office:** Let the dal cool completely before transferring to a leak-proof lunch box. Store the rotis stacked and wrapped in aluminium foil or a separate box to keep them soft. Reheat the dal with a splash of water if needed; rotis can be eaten at room temperature or briefly warmed.
- **No afternoon slump:** Masoor dal is high in plant protein and fibre, which means a slow, steady energy release — ideal before an afternoon of work.
- **Vegan option:** Use oil instead of ghee in both the tadka and roti — it's just as tasty.
- **Make it faster on weeknights:** The dal (up to step 8) can be made the night before and refrigerated. Make fresh rotis in the morning — each one takes under 2 minutes once the dough is ready.
- **Dal too thick after cooling?** Masoor dal thickens as it sits. Add a few tablespoons of hot water when reheating and stir well to get it back to the right consistency.
